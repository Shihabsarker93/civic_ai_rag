\section{Final Specifications and Requirements}
The needs-analysis survey informed the informational scope. Required-document guidance was selected by 62/77 valid respondents (80.5\%), and payment/appointment information by 58/77 (75.3\%). These preferences map to searchable instructions and source-grounded explanations. Demand for online-form help (64/77, 83.1\%) and uploaded-document checking (56/77, 72.7\%) was also recorded, but automatic submission and document authentication are outside the implemented requirements.

\paragraph{Functional Requirements.}
The system must accept a question with a selected domain, retrieve relevant passages, generate a Bangla response and expose the evidence supplied to the model. It must also support model selection and record answers, routes and source identifiers for comparison. A targeted clarification path handles recognized cross-domain aggregate requests; it is not a complete classifier for every out-of-domain question.

\paragraph{Non-functional Requirements.}
Traceability, maintainability and reproducibility guide the implementation. Original files must remain separate from derivatives, evidence must remain linked to stable IDs, and changes must be reversible. Language and incomplete-output checks support usability, while saved configurations permit reproducible analysis. Local response time is measured rather than treated as a guaranteed real-time service level.

\paragraph{Software Requirements.}
The implementation uses Python, domain data files, configuration files, BGE-M3 embeddings, ChromaDB, BM25, a BGE reranker and Ollama. The browser interface provides domain and model selection. Model weights must be available locally for offline execution; network access is needed for initial downloads and following external source links. Qwen3:8b is explicitly requested in the main comparison, irrespective of default model names retained in other configurations.

\paragraph{Resource Requirements.}
Execution requires storage for the source archive, prepared corpus, indexes, model weights and experiment outputs. Available memory must support the selected embedding, reranking and generation components. Sequential batch execution and model sharing reduce concurrent resource demand. No minimum GPU specification is asserted because the project did not perform a hardware-capacity benchmark.

\section{Societal Impact}
Civic.ai is intended to help citizens understand published procedures, not replace government services. Bangla explanations and inspectable evidence can make a dispersed collection easier to navigate. The survey indicates perceived demand for such assistance, but the completed experiments do not measure changes in office visits, financial burden or dependence on intermediaries.

The trust responses reinforce this distinction. Under a hypothetical verified-information condition, 42/76 respondents (55.3\%) still preferred to recheck answers. A combined official-logo/website-link option was selected by 46/77 (59.7\%). Its wording does not identify separate preferences for logos and links or justify official branding. We consequently emphasize source inspection without implying institutional endorsement.

Incorrect guidance can waste time or cause an applicant to prepare the wrong documents. Fees and procedures should therefore remain associated with their source conditions. Missing URLs and unresolved original-to-derivative matches are visible limitations of the dataset, not details to hide from users.

\section{Environmental Impact}
Local inference consumes electricity and hardware resources. Reusing pretrained models avoids conducting foundation-model training within this project, but does not establish a low-carbon system. Long inference and evaluation runs can still accumulate resource use.

We reuse saved answers for evaluation and preserve checkpoints instead of repeatedly regenerating completed batches. These practices avoid redundant work; their energy benefit was not measured. No power logs or carbon accounting were collected, so latency is reported as an operational measure rather than converted into unsupported environmental claims.

\section{Ethical Issues}
Source traceability is important because civic guidance can affect practical decisions. Original documents and derivatives are preserved separately, while identifiers and available URLs allow a response to be inspected. A correct source link does not certify that the derived text is accurate, current or applicable to every citizen.

The evidence-only prompt aims to reduce unsupported generation, and the pipeline can return clarification when no applicable evidence remains. Nevertheless, models can combine unrelated requirements or omit conditions. The system exposes incomplete-output warnings and selected passages rather than presenting these safeguards as a full factual verifier. Document dates and audit flags also identify where freshness and applicability require further checking.

Privacy is addressed differently for the corpus and survey. Public-service instructions form the retrieval corpus; respondent experiences are not added to it. Survey reporting uses aggregate counts from consenting adult submissions and excludes raw demographics, timestamps and free-text accounts from the distributed thesis artifacts. Four consenting under-18 submissions and two missing-age submissions are held out of the primary analysis. Participant identity and repeat participation cannot be reliably established, so counts refer to submissions rather than verified unique citizens.

Local inference avoids sending each question and passage to a hosted generator in the recorded runs, but saved local logs can still contain sensitive user input. Their use in an operational deployment would require access and retention controls. No ethics approval or privacy certification is inferred from a consent checkbox or local execution alone.

\section{Standards - if applicable}
\textbf{Encoding.} UTF-8 storage and Unicode NFC normalization provide a consistent representation of Bangla and mixed-language text. Normalization standardizes encoding; it does not repair every OCR or spelling error.

\textbf{Documentation.} JSON/JSONL records, stable identifiers, source-relative paths and SHA-256 hashes support consistent data exchange and change detection. Missing URLs remain missing rather than being replaced with guessed addresses.

\textbf{Software.} Versioned code, configuration files and saved experiment artifacts support reproducibility. Matching the active corpus to its index is essential because retrieved IDs are resolved against the configured chunk map.

\textbf{Government documents.} Official titles, dates, forms and conditions are retained where present. Their preservation supports provenance but is not certification that a document is current or that redistribution is authorized. The study claims no formal government, security, accessibility or quality-management compliance audit.

\section{Economic Analysis}
The recorded generation runs use local Ollama rather than paid per-request inference. This removes hosted generation API charges for those runs, not all project costs. Hardware access, electricity, storage, document preparation and review remain resource inputs. The project did not collect a complete invoice, power or labour record, so an itemized monetary total cannot be reported reliably.

The economic trade-off is therefore described in terms of resources and workflow. Reusing pretrained models avoids a new training programme. Saved-answer evaluation avoids repeating generation, while checkpointed batches preserve completed work after interruptions. On the other hand, sequential local inference limits how many experiments can be completed in the available time.

For a measured run, an energy charge could be calculated as
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{energy}}=\frac{P_{\mathrm{avg}}t}{1000}\,p,
\end{equation}
where $P_{\mathrm{avg}}$ is average power in watts, $t$ is elapsed hours and $p$ is the tariff per kWh. This equation defines a cost model; it is not a measured cost result. Total attributable cost would also include hardware, storage and documented labour.

The survey provides a separate view of stated willingness to pay. Of 78 valid adult responses, 33 (42.3\%) selected free-only use and 39 (50.0\%) would pay if the service were fast and secure; six supplied other responses. These preferences are neither purchases nor a revenue forecast. Consequently, our economic contribution is a locally executable and inspectable workflow, not a demonstrated financial saving for citizens.
