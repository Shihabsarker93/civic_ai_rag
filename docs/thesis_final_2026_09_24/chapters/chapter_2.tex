\section{Preliminaries}
This section introduces the concepts needed to understand our design. Preparing knowledge, finding applicable passages and generating an explanation are distinct activities; they should not be treated as one model operation.

\paragraph{Retrieval-Augmented Generation.}
RAG supplies external evidence to a language model at inference time \cite{lewis2020rag}. A simplified formulation is $a=G(q,E_q)$, where $q$ is the question, $E_q$ is the evidence and $a$ is the response. This makes the supporting information available separately from the model parameters. In Civic.ai, document updates do not require retraining the answer model, and the supplied context is retained with each saved result.

\paragraph{Document Understanding and Structured Text.}
Document conversion must preserve more than words: headings, tables and reading order help determine which conditions govern a statement. Docling is a document-understanding framework for structured PDF conversion, including layout analysis and table recognition, with machine-readable outputs such as Markdown \cite{docling}. In Civic.ai, the confirmed ingestion stage reads team-prepared Markdown and structured JSON; the archived records do not establish Docling as the converter used for those derivatives. We distinguish this relevant preprocessing framework from the verified implemented stages.

\paragraph{Dense Retrieval and Vector Storage.}
Dense retrieval encodes queries and passages into vectors and ranks them by proximity. BGE-M3 supports multilingual representations, motivating its use with our Bangla and English source material \cite{bgem3}. ChromaDB associates embeddings, document text and metadata through shared identifiers \cite{chroma}. In Civic.ai, BGE-M3 supplies dense vectors and a persistent Chroma collection per domain links results back to evidence. The storage choice supports local operation; ChromaDB does not generate answers.

\paragraph{Lexical Retrieval and Rank Fusion.}
BM25 uses term frequency, inverse document frequency and length normalization to rank lexical matches \cite{bm25}. It can retain exact service terms and form labels when those words matter. RRF merges ranked lists without assuming their raw scores share a common scale \cite{rrf}. In Civic.ai, a separate BM25 index complements dense search, and weighted RRF combines the rankings. Hybrid retrieval means combining these channels; RRF is our chosen combination method, not the only possible one.

\paragraph{Reranking and Evidence Selection.}
A cross-encoder evaluates a query and passage together, allowing finer interaction after initial retrieval. The multilingual BGE reranker is configured for this stage \cite{bgereranker}. Evidence selection then determines which candidates fit the service and action within a finite prompt budget. In Civic.ai, the selector is a heuristic function, not a second LLM or a human choosing passages during normal operation. Its trace makes decisions inspectable, but does not certify relevance.

\paragraph{Local Generation and Evaluation.}
Ollama provides a local model-serving interface \cite{ollama}. Qwen3 supports thinking and non-thinking operation \cite{qwen3}; the principal experiment uses the latter. RAGAS supplies model-assisted measures of RAG outputs \cite{ragas}. In Civic.ai, generation and saved-answer evaluation are separate processes: evaluating an existing response does not require generating it again. Answer support and relevance are also distinguished from passage-ranking quality.

\section{Review of Existing Research}
Existing work addresses complementary parts of the problem. General RAG provides the retrieval-generation foundation, multilingual systems address language coverage, and Bangla-focused research examines context use and verification. These contributions inform our design but do not remove the need to collect and structure applicable civic-service evidence.

\paragraph{Bilingual Retrieval and NextRAG.}
NextRAG is our principal architectural inspiration. It studies bilingual financial question answering using dense retrieval, BM25 and Dirichlet-smoothed query-likelihood retrieval, followed by weighted RRF, reranking and language-aware generation \cite{nextrag}. Its relevance is methodological: heterogeneous Bangla and English documents motivate complementary retrieval channels. Financial questions are not treated as a government-service benchmark or as directly comparable results.

Compared with NextRAG's three retrieval channels, CivicRAG uses two: BGE-M3 dense retrieval and BM25. We retain fusion and reranking while introducing our civic corpus, linked search/evidence representations and applicability selection. This is an adaptation rather than a replication of the paper's data, models or reported scores.

\paragraph{Bangla Context Use and Verification.}
TraSe investigates Bangla question answering through translative prompting and answer selection, including automatic and human-in-the-loop retrieval \cite{trase}. In contrast, HybridRAG-BN combines retrieval with a fine-tuned verification stage \cite{hybridragbn}. The former helps distinguish context limitations from generation limitations; the latter emphasizes verification after retrieval. They address related problems through different designs.

For Civic.ai, the distinction between manually selected and automatically retrieved evidence motivated small diagnostic trials. Better generation with manually chosen passages does not establish that those passages will be selected automatically. We do not implement TraSe's translation-and-selection architecture or train HybridRAG-BN's verifier. Our selector instead uses service and action cues before generation, keeping the additional runtime mechanism relatively simple while leaving its decisions open to evaluation.

Long-context research also shows that information placement can affect model performance \cite{lostmiddle}. Our evidence-order trials examine a related practical concern using known civic questions. This motivates examining both context content and ordering; it does not prove that ordering alone explains every error or that our small diagnostic reproduces the published study.

\paragraph{Multilingual Models and Document Infrastructure.}
BGE-M3 supports dense, sparse and multi-vector retrieval \cite{bgem3}. We use only its dense mode; CivicRAG's sparse component is BM25. This separates multilingual semantic representation from an interpretable lexical channel. ChromaDB provides persistent vector and document storage rather than a new ranking algorithm \cite{chroma}. These components support the dataset contribution but are not architectural novelty by themselves.

Qwen3, Qwen2.5 and Llama 3 provide locally deployable model families \cite{qwen3,qwen25,llama3card}. Our experiments concern the specific Ollama models requested in each run; local \code{llama3} is not NextRAG's Llama 3.1 variant. We selected Qwen3:8b for the principal paired comparison after project-level diagnostic runs, without claiming it is the best model for Bangla generally. Fixing the generator allows a clearer comparison of the retrieval-and-selection configurations.

\paragraph{Evaluation of Retrieval and Answers.}
RAGAS distinguishes context-supported statements from answer relevance \cite{ragas}. An answer can repeat evidence accurately while discussing the wrong task. We therefore use saved contexts and responses for answer-level measures, and ranked chunk IDs with relevance labels for retrieval-level measures. Our custom binary context-relevance measure is identified separately from standard RAGAS metrics \cite{ragasdocs}.

The current retrieval study includes assistant-reviewed pooled labels, not independently human-verified ground truth. Reference-answer similarity likewise requires reference answers; agreement between two generated outputs cannot substitute for them. These distinctions enable measurement without treating every available score as a correctness estimate.

Taken together, existing research provides established retrieval, generation and evaluation methods. The remaining project-specific gap is a structured, traceable collection of civic-service information and an implementation that preserves service conditions from documents to responses. Civic.ai addresses this gap through dataset development and an evaluated integration of existing methods, not a new general-purpose RAG architecture.

\section{Summary of Key Findings}
Three findings guide the design. First, multilingual semantic search and lexical matching address different retrieval needs, but their combination requires evaluation. Second, document structure and applicability matter alongside ranking: even a capable generator can receive the wrong procedure. Third, answer support, relevance and retrieval coverage describe different qualities.

These findings lead to the requirements in Chapter 3: preserve source structure, expose evidence, keep the pipeline modular and compare configurations under controlled conditions. They place dataset development at the centre of our contribution. The following chapters explain how these requirements are implemented and how the recorded experiments are interpreted.
